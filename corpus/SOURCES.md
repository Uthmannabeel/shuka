## Caveats

- **Page counts / substance.** Verified set: ~850 pages (maize-legume guide 88, Save and Grow Cassava 142, Save and Grow in Practice 124, tomato IPM 205, cassava facilitator guide 33, rice handbook 36, fall armyworm FFS guide 139, tomato value-addition manual 83). Unverified set adds ~213 pages. Total ~1,063 pages — within the 300–1500 target.
- **Fall armyworm.** The ASHC maize-legume guide (2016) predates the fall armyworm invasion of Africa; the FAO 2018 FFS guide (fao-fall-armyworm-maize-ffs-guide.pdf) is the corpus's authoritative FAW diagnosis/management source and should be preferred for FAW questions.
- **Tomato coverage is still the weakest on production agronomy.** The only verified tomato *production/IPM* document (FAO Tomato IPM Ecological Guide, 2000) was written for FAO's Asia vegetable IPM programme; the agronomy and pest ecology largely transfer, but it is not West Africa-specific and is dated. The verified IITA I-Youth manual (2025) is Nigeria-specific and CC-BY-4.0 but covers value addition/processing with only a brief cultivation overview. No Africa-specific, openly-licensed tomato production guide was found on CGSpace (the one CC-BY-4.0 "Good practices for tomato cultivation" item is Sri Lanka-specific). The Nigeria-specific tomato production bulletin (NAERLS) is license-unverified. IITA's "Integrated pest management in vegetable production: a guide for extension workers in West Africa" (2010) was downloaded, found to state "No reproduction... without written permission of the publisher", and deleted — it is NOT openly licensed.
- **Rice caveat.** The verified rice items are a Uganda-oriented smallholder handbook (CC-BY-4.0) and the rice chapters of FAO Save and Grow in Practice. The best practical West-African rice manuals (AfricaRice lowland/upland handbooks, Nigeria-focused) carry no license statement and sit in `unverified/`.
- **FAO licensing nuance.** Only FAO documents published after ~2017 carry an explicit CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO statement. The three FAO documents here predate that; the two Save and Grow titles print FAO's standard permission notice (non-commercial reproduction allowed with attribution), which was verified directly in each PDF. The 2000 tomato guide has no in-document notice; it relies on the FAO Knowledge Repository's Open Access policy and FAO's site terms (non-commercial copying permitted). All three are fine for a non-commercial offline assistant; none permits commercial redistribution.
- **CC BY 3.0 on the maize-legume guide explicitly allows commercial use** — it is the most permissive document in the corpus, alongside the two CC-BY-4.0 CGSpace items.
- **AfricaRice PDFs have a broken font encoding** in parts (some text extracts Caesar-shifted by 2 characters, e.g. "YJKEJ" = "which"); if they are later cleared and ingested, extraction quality should be checked page by page or OCR used.
- All 15 files were verified to start with `%PDF` and contain extractable text (no HTML error pages, no image-only scans).
